# Licensing and purchase model

## Proposed customer offer

The intended offer is a **one-time license purchase** for one named business. It gives that business a perpetual, non-transferable right to install, run, configure, and modify the CRM for its own business and its own products or projects. It does not give the buyer ownership of the source code, or permission to resell, sublicense, redistribute, or provide the CRM itself to other businesses as a service.

The proposed price is **USD $199 for the first 10 paid business licenses**, then **USD $299 per business**. This is the CRM license price only; hosting, hands-on installation, and custom integration are separate. Show the actual charge and any required taxes at checkout.

The private GitHub repository is for product development. Customers should receive a versioned installation package and the final customer license terms after purchase, not collaborator access to the source repository.

## Free internal use

The product owner and a partner specifically designated by the owner may use the CRM without a purchase fee for projects each owns or controls. Record the partner designation in a private written authorization. This internal-use exception does not extend to the partner's clients, customers, or other third parties, and it does not permit resale or redistribution.

The partner may be added to the private GitHub repository as a collaborator when source access is needed. GitHub repository access and the software-use authorization are separate: a collaborator invitation does not itself grant broader rights.

## Before accepting payment

1. Set the one-time price and identify the legal business that owns the product.
2. Have a lawyer prepare or review customer terms for the countries where the CRM will be sold. The final terms should state the authorized legal entity, permitted installation and integration scope, non-transferability, no-resale/no-redistribution restriction, and remedies and governing law.
3. Decide whether updates, support, additional installations, employee accounts, contractors, or customer-facing integrations are included in the one-time price.
4. Create a checkout and fulfillment process: confirm payment, record the purchaser's acceptance, issue a purchase-specific license document, and deliver a versioned installer package.
5. Decide whether to rely on the agreement and controlled delivery initially or implement signed license activation. The current app has no purchase verification or license-key enforcement.
